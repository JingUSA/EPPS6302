1. Public discourse: the 2021 Biden–Xi summit

The archived dataset contains 14,520 nonempty tweets, including 9,059 repeated text rows (62.4%). The leading terms are biden, xi, and summit; taiwan appears 1,578 times. Retaining repeats describes circulation rather than unique opinions. Hashtag co-occurrence links #biden and #china (439 tweets), whereas mention clusters connect media accounts and other public actors. These are within-tweet co-occurrences, not interpersonal communication networks. Keyword selection, a restricted collection window, retweets, and multilingual content limit representativeness. Users and media organizations produced the posts for online audiences; their frequencies cannot measure population-level support for policy.

2.1. CPD data note

Source and permission. GovInfo CPD is compiled by the Office of the Federal Register (NARA) from official materials. Collection used the documented GovInfo API with a locally stored key, not webpage scraping. GovInfo’s copyright notice generally identifies federal government works as public domain, while cautioning that third-party material can remain protected. Only metadata, aggregate results, short KWIC examples and logs are published here; full documents and the key remain private.

Query and retrieval. November 26, 2026 (CST); date window January 20, 2021–November 26, 2026, inclusive.

collection:CPD AND "Xi Jinping" AND publishdate:range(2021-01-20,2026-11-26)

3. Inaugural addresses, 1945–2025

The 21 speeches (10 Democratic, 11 Republican) come from data_corpus_inaugural; version 4.3.1 contains the corrected 2025 Trump address and no exact duplicate texts. Inaugurations are ceremonial speeches written for national audiences, not random observations of ordinary political language. Roosevelt (1945) is shortest (555 tokens), while Trump (2025) is longest (2,909). Moving-average TTR (100-token windows) ranges from .640 to .711, mitigating ordinary TTR’s length dependence. Flesch scores range from 48.8 (Truman, 1949) to 73.2 (Biden, 2021); higher scores indicate easier estimated reading. Cosine similarity is highest for Reagan’s two speeches (.725), and lowest for Roosevelt 1945 and Trump 2017 (.224). Republican keyness highlights government and freedom, Democratic keyness democracy and change. These exploratory word associations are not independent tests of ideological differences.

4. What does Wordfish measure?

Wordfish (Slapin and Proksch 2008) is an unsupervised, one-dimensional Poisson model of word counts:

yij ∼ Poisson (λij),   log λij = αi + ψj + βjθi.

Document effects αi adjust overall verbosity, word effects ψj capture baseline frequency, and βj measures discrimination along document position θi. Conditional independence and a common one-dimensional structure are simplifying assumptions. The model uses 2,093 features across 21 addresses. Setting dir orders Roosevelt 1945 below Trump 2025 to fix the otherwise arbitrary sign; it does not define ideology. Positions correlate strongly with year (r = .874) but weakly with Republican affiliation (r = .098). Earlier words such as joint and Europe contrast with later words such as radical and Obama. The dimension appears largely temporal, reflecting changing vocabulary and historical context rather than a validated left–right scale. Validation requires independent political-position measures, examination of high-discrimination terms in context, robustness to preprocessing and genre, and alternative scaling specifications. The plotted intervals reflect model-based uncertainty, not uncertainty about construct validity.
